# Policy Context

Agent Authority Infrastructure is not a substitute for legal, compliance, RMF, or ATO review. It is a framework for producing better action-level evidence for those processes.

## OMB AI Guidance

Recent OMB AI guidance pushes federal agencies to accelerate AI use while maintaining governance, risk management, acquisition discipline, and public trust.

AAI is relevant because agentic workflows introduce action-level questions that model documentation and ordinary system logs may not answer:

- What was the agent authorized to do?
- What decision was made before execution?
- What evidence exists after execution?
- What was denied or escalated?

## NIST AI RMF

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework emphasizes governance, mapping, measurement, and management of AI risks.

AAI can support AI RMF-aligned narratives by producing evidence related to runtime controls, action boundaries, accountability, and monitoring.

## NIST AI Agent Work

NIST has moved directly into AI-agent security, identity, authorization, and interoperability questions. AAI is positioned as a practical framework for the action boundary those conversations require.

## RMF / ATO Review

AAI does not grant an authorization, replace an Authorizing Official, or certify a system.

AAI can help produce evidence that supports security review and RMF/ATO readiness:

- Policy definition
- Runtime decision records
- Denied-action evidence
- Human-review records
- Signed receipt verification
- Closeout evidence packs

## Buyer-Safe Claim

AAI supports security-review and RMF/ATO readiness. It does not claim customer authorization, FedRAMP authorization, FIPS validation, or agency approval.
